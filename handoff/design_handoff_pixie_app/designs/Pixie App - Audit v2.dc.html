Pixie Rides · audit v2 94 screens · 64 turns · July 2026
Re-audit · against the rules you set

Account-only broke four screens. The rest held.

Nine rules have been decided since the first audit. All 94 screens re-read against them. The eight numbered findings below are what's left; everything from v1 that was swept is confirmed still fixed. One rule — no account, no access — did most of the damage, because it changes what a screen is allowed to show rather than what it says.

The rules being audited against
1. No account, no access in the app
2. Direct to driver on trip day, dispatch otherwise
3. 15 min = human confirm · 20 min = driver hold
4. Cheaper change refunds the difference at re-confirm
5. Airline cancellation waives the fee automatically
6. Spine: Requested → Confirmed → Paid → Driver assigned → Complete
7. Driver-app orange = the one action that advances the run
8. The sign carries the booker's first and last name
9. Sign mode is full-screen landscape, with the flight and its landed time

Must fix — account-only contradicts these

Each of these was designed under the old assumption that a signed-out client could still see their trip.

01 Two different screens now answer "request received"

6a hands a signed-out client a "Track this trip" button and treats the account as a nicety — "Want it saved for next time?". 64b says the opposite: this is exactly where the app stops. They can't both ship, and 6a is the one that's wrong now.

Conflict: 6a vs 64b
Fix: retire 6a. 64b is the screen — same reassurance, same recap, but "Sign in to track this request" instead of a track button that can't work.
02 There is no such thing as "create an account" any more

33a offers "New to Pixie? Create an account" as a separate path, and "Sign in with email" as an alternative. But under the model in turn 64 the phone number is the account: requesting creates the record, the six-digit code claims it. A new client who taps "create an account" has nothing to create, and an email sign-in can't match a request that only knows a mobile number.

Where: 33a · echoed by 6a and 62c's "save your trips"
Fix: one field, one path. Drop both alternates; the sub-line becomes "First time? The same number works — we'll make your account as you sign in."
03 A forgot-password screen for a product with no passwords

54a leads with "No password needed" and then offers "Email a reset link" underneath — a reset for a credential that doesn't exist. Its one genuinely useful line is the last one: changed your number? Call dispatch. That case is real and it's the only thing on the screen worth keeping.

Where: 54a
Fix: delete the reset path and rename the screen "Can't get the code" — resend, then call dispatch. It stops being an auth screen and becomes a recovery one.
04 The two original home screens have nowhere to sign in

1a puts sign-in behind the Account tab; 1b buries it in a top-left menu. Rule 1 makes the chip a first-screen requirement, because the likeliest visitor is a returning customer with a trip they can't otherwise reach. 1a and 1b are turn-1 explorations, so the risk is low — but they're still the only two full home-screen concepts in the file.

Superseded by: 64a
Fix: mark 1a/1b as superseded, or add the chip to both so nobody builds from an old shell.

Drift — smaller, but they'll be asked about

05 The cancellation waiver is automatic on one screen and manual on the other

20a now says the fee doesn't apply and "you don't have to ask." 46a still says "message us and we'll move your ride… or refund you in full." Same policy, two levels of effort. 46a should match 20a: we detect it and waive it; message us only if you want a different flight.

06 Receipts are emailed to an address nobody ever collected

45a shows "Receipt after each ride — emailed to dana@reyes.family" as on by default. But booking (64a–b) collects a name and a mobile, and sign-in is a texted code — there is no screen anywhere that asks for an email. Either receipts text a link like everything else, or Account gains an optional email field and 45a reads "not set — add one" until it's filled.

07 Two sign modes still exist

Rule 9 made the sign full-screen landscape (63c), but 28b is still there as a portrait card with "Done — they found me." A driver shown both will ask which one is the app. Retire 28b; 63c's tap-to-exit replaces its button.

08 The link and the app now grant different access, and it's nowhere written down

62b gives a signed-out traveller the price, the spine, the driver and change options — from a URL. 64b says the app can show a signed-out traveller nothing. Both are defensible (the URL is a credential, the app has no way to know who's holding the phone) but stated side by side they read as a contradiction. Write the rule once: the texted link is read access to one trip; the app is the account. Then 62b is the exception on purpose, not an oversight.

Still open from v1

A cheaper change still has no screen

Rule 4 is decided — refund the difference at re-confirm — but 17b only handles +$35. The mirror screen doesn't exist, and it's the one that involves giving money back.

Group (16) and cruise (17), parked as v1.1

Unchanged since v1: 53a/53b stop at the price, 49a/49b have no price reveal, no receipt and no ship-delayed screen.

New: what if the number isn't hers?

Claim-by-phone is elegant when it works. It has three failure modes with no screens: a request typed with a typo'd number, a trip booked by a spouse or assistant on their own phone, and a customer who changes number between trips. The last one 54a half-covers.

Confirmed still clean

Re-checked, not assumed — including everything swept after v1.

Messaging. Every remaining "Message Marcus" is trip-day (58a, 8a, 49b, 61a, 2a); 10b routes to dispatch.
15 vs 20 minutes. No screen uses 15 for the hold any more; the two numbers no longer collide.

What I'd do

1. Retire, don't rewrite. 01, 03, 04 and 07 are deletions — 6a, 54a, 1a/1b and 28b are superseded by screens that already exist. That's the cheapest hour in this document.
2. Fix 33a properly. 02 is the only must-fix that needs new copy, and it's the first screen a returning customer sees.
3. Answer the email question. 06 is small but it decides whether Account has an email field at all.
4. Then the claim-by-phone edges. Wrong number, someone else's phone, changed number. Elegant mechanics fail quietly, and this one currently has no failure screens at all.
